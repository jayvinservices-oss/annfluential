## 1. The business as Ann has framed it

- **Name:** "AI Finance Operations" (also written "AI Finance Ops"). On 1 Nov Ann decided to have **no brand name**. Everything runs under **Ann James**, the way Boucher runs under his own name. Instagram stays Annfluential. The handover note says: "Do not raise brand names again until she does." Brand ideas held in reserve in 03: The Lean Close (Claude's pick), The Controller's Desk, Finance on Autopilot, Close Club. Kit is owned by Jayvin behind the scenes, and "Jayvin's name appears nowhere a subscriber sees". A website can carry either the Jayvin Consulting name or Ann's name, and that choice is deferred until launch (02).
- **Positioning:** "Controls-first AI." A Chartered Accountant sets up a small company's finance and admin back office to run on the Microsoft 365 tools it already pays for (Outlook, Excel, Teams, Power Automate) plus Claude. A human approves anything that moves money or changes the books. Pitch: *"For $5,000 I set up your finance back office to run on AI, safely, and your team gets hours back every week."* Proof line: "Most people selling AI automation are technical. I'm a Chartered Accountant. I set it up the way an auditor would want to see it." Draft website headline: "Your finance back office, run on AI. Set up by a Chartered Accountant."
- **Target clients:** owner-managed **for-profit small businesses**: 10 to 100 staff in 02, narrowed to 5 to 50 staff using Microsoft 365 in 08, 13 and 15. Two buyers: the **owner**, who signs, and the **finance or admin lead** (bookkeeper, controller, office manager), who is the way in. Best-fit segments (13): trades and construction, professional services, property and condo management, clinics and health practices, distributors and wholesale. Geography: GTA (Toronto, Peel, York), about 82,000 businesses with 5 to 99 staff (estimate). Accounting firms, bookkeepers and managed IT providers are treated as **referral partners**, not rivals.
- **Problem solved:** hours of copy-paste each week (inbox triage, invoice intake and approvals, reconciliations, month-end close, owner reporting), plus "shadow AI": staff using personal ChatGPT on company data with no rules. Privacy is the top AI barrier small businesses name (StatCan), and controls-first is the answer to it. Expected benefit: 3 to 8 hours a week back, about $6,200 to $16,600 a year at $40 an hour, with payback in about 4 to 10 months (estimates).
- **Ann's motive (02, 00):** money that is her own, aimed at replacing a $130K salary (about USD 9K a month, roughly 2 setups plus 2 retainers a month). Her stated aim is to stay in a professional, B2B space. Employment context: contains employer specifics, omitted.

## 6. Conflicts with the draft v2 offer

Draft v2: monthly packages for small nonprofits and small businesses at CA$650 / 1,450 / 2,950, a CA$150/hour rate, and the working name "Northbook Finance, a service of Jayvin Consulting Inc."

1. **Brand name.** Ann decided on 1 Nov to use no brand and run under "Ann James", and the handover says not to raise brand names until she does. "Northbook Finance" goes against that decision unless Ann reopens it. The older shortlist (The Lean Close and others) did not include it, and no availability check has been done.
2. **Jayvin as the public entity.** The drafts keep Jayvin invisible to subscribers. A public Jayvin website is flagged as the most visible risk (employer-related details omitted). The drafts publish nothing until Ann decides. "A service of Jayvin Consulting Inc." puts Jayvin up front.
3. **Nonprofit targeting.** The drafts deliberately target **for-profit** small businesses. Every charity-specific feature (donations, receipting, funders, restricted funds) was stripped out of the client template (08). Under the traceability rule, content must not reveal her sector, and 04 says the brand is "small finance teams", not "nonprofit finance". Nonprofit finance is called a sharper wedge only "if you later go named and disclosed". Naming small nonprofits as a target is a direct conflict. (The 02 workflow menu does keep "grant or funder reporting support, for organisations that have it".)
4. **Pricing model.** The drafts sell a **fixed-scope project** ($1,000 audit, then a $5,000 one-time setup with handover, so the client runs it), with an optional Care retainer of **$750 to $1,500 a month**. They say "you never sell hours." Monthly packages as the main offer change the model:
   - $650 is below the Care floor.
   - $1,450 sits inside the Care range.
   - $2,950 is above any monthly price in the drafts.
   - There is no setup fee or audit in the v2 draft. The drafts rely on the $5,000 setup and the $1,000 credited audit.
5. **Hourly rate.** CA$150/hour clashes with the "fixed scope, never sell hours" principle. It fits the drafts' internal effective rate (about $152 an hour for the first client, $182 after) and the engagement letter's "$[rate] per hour" for out-of-scope changes. So $150 works as a change-request rate, not as a headline price.
6. **Scope of service.** The v2 packages read like ongoing finance work done for the client. The drafts are AI setup and handover, and the engagement letter explicitly excludes bookkeeping, tax filings, payroll processing and audit work. Ongoing bookkeeping or controller work would be a different service, and the drafts list it as competitor territory (Enkel and others, $300 to $1,500 a month).
7. **Timing and capacity.** The drafts switch selling on around **3 May 2027** and cap load at 1 to 2 clients while Ann is employed. A monthly retainer book of several clients does not fit that capacity, and any launch before the switch conflicts with the plan.
8. **Tool costs.** The drafts require the client to own and pay for Claude, M365 and Power Automate in its own name. The v2 packages must not bundle or resell licences.

**Points the v2 offer can reuse unchanged:** CAD pricing; the controls-first positioning; the ten controls; the handover pack, AI policy, skills pack and Pemberline demos; the Care-plan contents as the basis of a monthly tier; and the intake form and audit prompt.
