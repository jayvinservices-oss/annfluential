## The checklist (one page, Jayvin branding, send as PDF)
**Grant claim checklist: IRAP, CanExport and other government contributions**
Jayvin Consulting Inc. · info@jayvinconsulting.ca · General guidance only. Your contribution agreement always wins.

**Before you spend anything**
- Read the contribution agreement and note: project start and end dates, eligible cost categories, the
  reimbursement rate, claim frequency and due dates, the final report date, and the HST treatment.
- Spend nothing before the project start date. Costs dated earlier are usually not eligible.
- Set up a project code, class or job in your accounting software for this grant only.
- Pay project costs from the business account or card, never a personal one.
- Put every claim and report due date in a shared calendar with two reminders.

**Every month**
- Code every project cost to the grant as it is entered, with the cost category the funder uses.
- Keep the invoice and the proof of payment together (bank or card line showing it cleared).
- For salaries (IRAP): timesheets or time records for each person on the project, matching payroll.
- For contractors: signed agreement, invoices describing the project work, proof of payment.
- For travel and trade shows (CanExport): itinerary, receipts, boarding passes, and a note on the export purpose.
- Reconcile the bank and card accounts the project spends from.

**Before each claim**
- Run a report of costs coded to the grant for the claim period and tie it to the general ledger.
- Check every cost is inside the project dates, in an eligible category and within the approved budget line.
- Remove anything without a receipt, proof of payment or time record.
- Apply the reimbursement rate from the agreement, and HST only as the agreement allows.
- Keep a copy of exactly what was submitted, with the supporting schedule.

**After payment and at close-out**
- Record the payment received against the claim and note any costs the funder disallowed, with the reason.
- Track the final report and any results the funder asked you to measure (jobs, sales, export markets).
- Keep all records for at least six years, or longer if the agreement says so. Funders can audit after the project ends.

**Where claims usually get cut**
Costs before the start date · personal-card payments · missing timesheets · invoices that do not describe the
project work · spending outside the approved budget lines · late claims.
