## 2. Scanning for small businesses (Claude runs, Ann approves contact)
Public, legal sources that signal "growing and needs finance help":
| Source | Signal | How |
|---|---|---|
| Federal grants and contributions open data (IRAP, CanExport, FedDev, WES, Black Entrepreneurship Program) | New grant = claims, cost tracking, reports | Claude scans monthly; first scan 5 Nov |
| Ontario Business Registry and new incorporations | New companies need books set up | Monthly, by sector and GTA city |
| Licensed child care centres (Ontario open data) | CWELCC funding reports, payroll, tight margins | One scan, then quarterly |
| Allied health clinics (physio, dental, chiro college directories) | Owner-run, busy, HST and payroll | Pick 2 niches, 25 a month |
| Job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn): "bookkeeper", "part-time accountant", "finance manager" at small firms | They are hiring for the exact gap we fill | Weekly scan; pitch "a team for less than a hire" |
| Google Maps by niche and city | Reviews show growth (new locations, hiring) | Batch research by Claude |
| CRA charity data (40 already in the CRM as Later) | Fast growth, tiny team | Held until nonprofit outreach starts |
Every lead goes into the CRM with the reason it fits. Ann screens names against the exclusions (employer, its funders,
partners and suppliers, DNC, QT, Rainbow Railroad); the CRM also blocks those names.

## 3. Outreach rhythm (CASL compliant)
- Business email addresses published for that role only (CASL implied consent applies only when the address is
  conspicuously published, there is no "no unsolicited email" notice beside it, and the message is relevant to their
  role). Every email: sender name, Jayvin Consulting Inc., mailing address, one-click opt-out. No bought lists.
- 10 personalised emails a week to start (Claude drafts from the fit signal; Ann approves and sends from the practice
  email). Follow up once after 5 working days, once more after 10, then mark Lost or Later.
- The offer in the first email: a free 20-minute call, then the $1,000 health check (credited if they sign).
- Target funnel: 40 emails a month > 6 to 8 replies > 3 to 4 calls > 1 to 2 health checks > 1 client a month.

## 4. Other lead channels (cheapest and fastest first)
1. Ann's own network outside work: ICAN Canada members, MBA alumni, church and community groups, friends who own
   businesses. A short "I now help small businesses with their books" message. Usually the first 1 to 2 clients.
2. Referral partners: small audit and tax firms that do not want bookkeeping work, immigration consultants, business
   lawyers, IT support firms, grant writers. Offer: one month free per signed client (or 10% of year one).
3. QuickBooks ProAdvisor directory and Intuit's "find an accountant" (free after certification).
4. Google Business Profile + 5 reviews (QT first) + one article a fortnight on the site (IRAP claims, HST for small
   business, month-end close, cash flow forecast). These are the searches that bring inbound leads.
5. Free tools as lead magnets: AI Use Policy lite, 13-week cash forecast template. Email address in exchange.
   First one built: the "Comment GRANT" LinkedIn post and grant claim checklist (`marketing/lead-magnet-grant-checklist.md`,
   Drive: "Lead magnet: GRANT post and grant claim checklist").
6. Workshops: one free webinar a quarter ("Get your books grant-ready") through a library, Futurpreneur, Enterprise
   Toronto, a BIA or a Black or African business association. Small rooms convert well.
7. Communities: local Facebook and LinkedIn business groups, Reddit r/smallbusiness Canada threads. Answer questions,
   no hard selling.
8. Grant-program ecosystems: IRAP Industrial Technology Advisors and CanExport alumni groups know which firms
   struggle with claims; ask for introductions once there is a case study.
9. Paid search only after reviews exist (start CA$300 a month on "bookkeeper Toronto" and "IRAP claim help").
